## What the Lesson JSON Actually Is

The `M1T1L1-comprehensive-analysis.json` is not raw material that needs
transformation — it's already a `.lesson` file in spirit. The
`day_by_day_slide_deck_maps` section is a pre-authored 12-slot sequence with
exact content strings, sentence stems, timing, TEKS tags, and step badges for
both days. The content pipeline work Opus described as future work is already
done for this lesson.

That changes the question from *"how do we extract content from the JSON"* to
*"how do we route it."*

Additional pre-authored slots at the top level of the JSON:
- `bell_ringer_spiral_suggestions` — Day 1 and Day 2 Bell Ringer problems
- `hook_suggestions` — Day 1 and Day 2 hook prompts
- `3b_activity_suggestions` — Participation strategy options per day
- `sentence_stems_collection` — All 3A Talk stems organized by activity
- `exit_ticket_suggestions` — Day 1 and Day 2 exit ticket prompts
- `graphic_organizer_template` — 3B participation graphic organizer
- `assessment_checkpoints` — Formative assessment checkpoints

## The Structural Gap: No "Current Lesson" Pointer

Right now the warm-up system has no way to answer the question: **which lesson
is active today?** The `active_warmups.json` knows what each student is working
on. The `attendance/YYYY-MM-DD.json` knows who checked in. But there's no
authoritative source of truth for "today is G8_M1_T1_L1, day 1."

**This is the first architectural piece to add** — call it
`current_lesson.json`:

```json
{
  "lesson_id": "G8_M1_T1_L1",
  "lesson_day": 1,
  "date": "2026-09-01",
  "json_path": "~/Bluebonnet/Grade8/M1T1L1-comprehensive-analysis.json"
}
```

Once this exists, every other component can derive from it. The teacher (or
eventually a cron system) sets this at the start of each day. Everything else
is just reading it.

## Two Content Flows That Need the Lesson JSON

### Flow 1: The DM Tutoring Channel (`pre_llm_call`)

This is what already exists. The bot sends a warm-up, the student replies, and
`pre_llm_call` injects context into the LLM before it responds.

Right now, the injected context is one warm-up problem. With the lesson JSON,
it should become the **full instructional context** for that student's
conversation:

- Current TEKS (8.10A)
- Essential question
- Key terms being introduced today
- Common misconceptions to watch for
- The Bell Ringer problem the student received

This transforms the DM bot from "I know your warm-up problem" into "I know
where you are in the lesson arc." The LLM's tutoring becomes grounded in the
actual curriculum.

**Design constraint:** The Bell Ringer content should come directly from
`bell_ringer_spiral_suggestions.day_X` in the JSON. These are already authored
text problems. Some are visual (e.g., "draw two shapes that look the same but
one is bigger") — text-only problems map cleanly to the DM, visual ones
require either description-only or image delivery.

### Flow 2: The Class-Period Room (Teacher-Triggered)

The `day_by_day_slide_deck_maps.day_X.slide_map` is already a 12-slot sequence
that maps to the campus cycle. Slides 5–9 are DI blocks. Slides 6 and 8 are
3A Talk prompts with sentence stems. Slide 10 is 3B Participation. Slide 11 is
Practice. Slide 12 is Closure with the exact exit ticket text.

**For a prototype, teacher-triggered is the right answer.** It's safer
pedagogically — the teacher controls pacing, can hold on a slide, and the
bot's job is just to know what comes next and what to say when asked. The full
orchestrator with timers and Teacher Radar is the end-state but adds complexity
before the content flow is validated.

### Where the Two Flows Connect

The `!here` command is already the junction point. With `current_lesson.json`
in place:
1. Attendance is recorded (unchanged)
2. DM room is created (unchanged)
3. Warm-up is sent from `bell_ringer_spiral_suggestions.day_X` instead of
   the hardcoded pool
4. `active_warmups.json` seeding and `pre_llm_call` injection remain
   structurally identical — just the content source changes

## What the Lesson JSON Provides Per Cycle Step

| Campus Cycle Step | What the JSON Provides | Bot Action |
|-|-|-|
| **Bell Ringer** | `bell_ringer_spiral_suggestions.day_X` — authored text problems | Send in DM on `!here` |
| **Hook** | `hook_suggestions.day_X` + `slide_map[2].content` | Post to team room (teacher-triggered or timer) |
| **DI blocks** | `slide_map` slides 5, 7, 9 — full content strings | Post sequentially to team room |
| **3A Talk** | `slide_map` slides 6, 8 — prompt + sentence stems | Post to team room; collect student replies |
| **3B Participation** | `3b_activity_suggestions.options[day]` — full strategy with prompts | Post to team room; run poll or reaction |
| **Practice** | `slide_map` slide 11 — questions and sentence stems | Post to team room |
| **Closure / Exit Ticket** | `exit_ticket_suggestions.day_X` + `slide_map` slide 12 | Post to team room; collect via DM or reaction |
| **DM tutoring (any time)** | TEKS, essential question, key terms, misconceptions | Injected into `pre_llm_call` context |

## The Content Boundary to Enforce Now

One principle from the infrastructure design that applies immediately: **the bot
should never generate lesson content using an LLM.** The JSON has the content;
the LLM's job is only to respond to students in the DM room. When the bot posts
to a team room (Hook, DI, 3A Talk prompt), it should be posting strings
directly from the lesson JSON — no LLM involved in that path. The
`verbatim: true` constraint from the spec applies here even before the spec
exists formally.

This is important because the lesson JSON has already made the pedagogical
decisions (exactly which sentence stem, exactly what the 3A question is).
Letting the LLM paraphrase those is the "fidelity drift" risk Opus flagged.
The content posts are **dispatches, not generations.**

## The Minimal Wiring Sequence

Given the existing infrastructure, the path from "hardcoded pool" to
"lesson JSON" is:

1. **Add `current_lesson.json`** — Teacher sets this manually to start; cron
   sets it later when school calendar awareness is added.

2. **`!here` reads the lesson JSON** — The hook handler reads
   `current_lesson.json`, loads the lesson JSON, pulls
   `bell_ringer_spiral_suggestions.day_X` as the warm-up content. This replaces
   the `_pick_warmup()` hardcoded pool.

3. **Expand `pre_llm_call` context** — The plugin injects the full lesson
   metadata (TEKS, essential question, key terms, misconceptions) alongside the
   warm-up problem.

4. **`!next` command (teacher-only)** — Reads the lesson JSON's `slide_map` in
   sequence and posts the next slot's content to the team room.

Steps 1–3 extend what's already working. Step 4 is the first piece of the
Lesson Orchestrator without building the full state machine.

## Our Response

> **Agreed on:** `current_lesson.json` as the foundational architecture piece.
> The two-flow distinction (DM tutoring vs team room delivery) is the right
> frame. Teacher-triggered pacing is correct for a prototype. The verbatim
> constraint from Opus is correctly applied.
>
> **Refinement:** Step 4 (`!next`) is bigger than it sounds — the bot needs
> to know which team rooms exist, who's in them, and track sequential state
> across rooms. That's the beginning of the Lesson Orchestrator. Steps 1–3
> can be deployed without touching the team room flow at all.
>
> **Next practical step:** Take step 1 alone — add `current_lesson.json` and
> wire the `!here` hook to read Bell Ringer content from the lesson JSON
> instead of the hardcoded pool. That's a self-contained change that validates
> the entire routing pattern before building the classroom delivery side.
